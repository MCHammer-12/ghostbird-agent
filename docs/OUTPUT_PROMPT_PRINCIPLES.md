# Output prompt principles

These principles are derived from Ryan Cook's public descriptions of Ghostbird's process, his writer-evaluation criteria, and two public interviews. They are inputs for the output prompts, not a replacement for Ghostbird's internal methods.

## Core model

Every post should combine:

1. A client-specific truth or story.
2. A clear audience and business purpose.
3. An agency-selected content framework.
4. The client's natural spoken and approved written voice.
5. Evidence references for every client-specific claim.

Frameworks provide structure. Personal details prevent the structure from becoming generic.

## Post brief

Before drafting, establish:

- Client and intended audience
- Primary post goal: reach, trust, or conversion
- Topic, stance, or story
- Supporting metrics, quotes, and anecdotes
- One or two personal details that make the post recognizably theirs
- Relevant industry context or research
- Preferred framework, if the agency supplies one
- Candidate visual asset
- Voice profile and hard prohibitions

If the evidence is not strong enough for a specific post, return a clarification or interview question instead of filling the gap with invention.

## Drafting behavior

- Start with a strong, specific hook grounded in the selected angle.
- Maintain attention with useful transitions or midhooks when the post is long enough to need them.
- Use short paragraphs and accessible language unless the client's approved voice indicates otherwise.
- Build a satisfying story arc when the source material contains a story.
- Preserve specific spoken details, phrasing, humor, opinions, and controlled imperfections that make the client sound human.
- Use the selected framework without making the post feel templated.
- Keep the post focused on one funnel role.
- Suggest an image only when it strengthens the story or idea.
- Do not fabricate personal details, opinions, results, or endorsements.
- Attach evidence references directly to the claims or suggestions they support.

## Authenticity test

Fail or revise the draft when:

- Another executive could publish it unchanged.
- It contains no personal detail, real opinion, or client-specific evidence.
- The framework is more visible than the person.
- The prose has been polished into a generic AI register.
- It misrepresents spoken language as a verified fact.
- It optimizes for engagement while weakening the client's real position.

A useful test from Ryan's public comments is whether the post includes a small detail the client might naturally text to a friend but a general model would never know to invent.

## Relationship orientation

Treat LinkedIn as a relationship and authority channel rather than a one-post lead machine.

- Early posts may primarily establish familiarity and beliefs.
- Trust-building posts should reveal expertise, judgment, stories, and generosity.
- Conversion posts can make the business relevance explicit after trust exists.
- Calls to action should fit the funnel role and the client's normal style.

Do not promise immediate results. Ghostbird publicly describes audience building and lead generation as an iterative, multi-month process.

## Ideation behavior

Generate ideas from the intersection of:

- Current client evidence
- Industry developments
- Competitor or category conversations
- Proven agency formats
- The client's business goals
- Gaps in the current content mix

Return distinct angles rather than rewordings. Label each with its funnel role, evidence strength, why it fits the client, and any missing detail that would improve it. The agency can replace this default ideation recipe with its own workflow.

## Output evaluation

Score or review:

- Evidence grounding
- Client specificity
- Voice match
- Hook strength
- Readability
- Story completeness
- Industry accuracy
- Funnel fit
- Framework fit without obvious templating
- Visual opportunity
- Unsupported-claim count
